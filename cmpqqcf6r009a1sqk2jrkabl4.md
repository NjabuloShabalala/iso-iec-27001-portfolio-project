There's a phrase that gets used a lot in the articles and YouTube videos that I've watched, and it's that "identity is the new perimeter." The first time I encountered it, it sounded like marketing language. The more I've learned about it and started grasping the context that it was being used in, the more I think I'm able to confidently agree.

Microsoft Entra ID is Microsoft's identity and access management platform. If you're not familiar with what that means, here's the simplest way I can put it: every time someone logs into a work system, something has to decide whether they're who they say they are, and whether they're allowed to be there. Entra ID is what makes that decision, and it's what enforces the rules around it.

The Applied Skills credential I completed covers the fundamentals of how that works from users, groups, roles, and the policies that govern access. But the concept that reframed how I think about security was Zero Trust. The traditional model assumed that anyone already inside the network could be trusted, since they were already inside, of course. Zero Trust flips that. It assumes nothing, verifies everything, and grants the least amount of access necessary to get the job done. I know it's crazy, and honestly sounds paranoid until you consider that most breaches involve credentials that were already legitimate, someone's password was stolen, or an account was compromised, and the attacker simply walked in through the front door.

That's why identity matters so much. The perimeter, which was the old idea of a secure boundary around a corporate network, effectively doesn't exist anymore. People work from home, from coffee shops, from their phones. The question is no longer just "are you inside the building" but "are you who you claim to be, and should you have access to this specific thing, right now."

Entra ID is how Microsoft answers that question at scale. Learning how it works gave me a clearer picture of why so much of modern security thinking starts with identity rather than ending with it.
