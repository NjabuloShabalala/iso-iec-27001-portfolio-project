Before I get into what the exam actually covered, I think it's worth being honest about what the exam isn't. The SC-900 is Microsoft's Security, Compliance, and Identity Fundamentals certification, which isn't a technical deep dive. The learning doesn't teach you how to configure a firewall or write a detection rule. What it does do, though, is give you the foundational language that you need and will come across throughout your Azure experience. And when you're coming from a non-tech-traditional background like mine, having the language matters more than people give it credit for.

My background is in Organisational Psychology and International Relations. Cybersecurity wasn't the initial plan, but the more I read about how organisations actually fail, not because of bad technology, but because of bad decisions, poor governance, and gaps in awareness. The more I realised that understanding people and understanding risk aren't that different from each other. The SC-900 was the bridge that let me start translating one into the other.

The exam covers three broad areas: security concepts, Microsoft's core security and compliance products, and identity, meaning how organisations control who has access to what, and why that matters. That last piece, identity, ended up being the one that stuck with me most. In a world where most breaches don't involve someone kicking down a physical door but rather someone walking through one they shouldn't have access to, identity is everything.

Passing the SC-900 didn't make me a security professional (yet anyway). What it did was confirm that I'm moving in the right direction and gave me a solid enough foundation to build something more substantial on top of.
