If I'm being honest, the SC-200 was the destination before I fully understood the road. The Microsoft Security Operations Analyst certification is the exam that sits closest to the actual work of a SOC analyst, which entails detecting threats, investigating incidents, and responding to attacks using Microsoft's security stack. Sentinel, Defender XDR, KQL, identity-based threats. It's not a fundamentals exam. It assumes you already know the landscape and asks you to operate within it.

That's why I didn't start there. I couldn't have, even if I wanted to. The one exam that i did take before the SC-200 was the SC-900, and this gave me the language, security concepts, compliance frameworks, and identity principles. Without it, half the SC-200 material would have been noise to me. The Applied Skills credentials pushed me further into the actual platforms. Defender XDR taught me how threats are detected and correlated across an environment. Purview grounded me in how data is classified and protected before a threat even materialises. Entra ID showed me why identity is where most attacks begin, and therefore where a lot of defensive thinking has to start, too.

KC7 is where the KQL is becoming something I can actually use rather than just describe. Every scenario I work through on that platform, I see as practice for the kind of querying the SC-200 will expect me to do fluently, almost second nature. Hunting through logs, building detection logic, following an attack chain through the data. The home lab I've tried building, even in its incomplete state, is teaching me something the courses can't: what it feels like to not understand something and have to work backwards until you do.

None of these things alone would be enough. But together they've been building a foundation that makes the SC-200 feel like a logical next step rather than a leap.
